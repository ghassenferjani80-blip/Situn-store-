# Commission model research notes

These are market benchmarks, not legal or universal pricing rules. Final rates should be reviewed for the countries and contracts used by SITUN.

- Realtor.com, “Who Pays the Real Estate Commission and Closing Costs”: https://www.realtor.com/advice/finance/realtor-fees-closing-costs/ — notes that traditional real-estate commission is often around 5–6%, but actual rates vary.
- Marketplace.org, “Real estate industry braces for commissions overhaul”: https://www.marketplace.org/story/2024/07/25/realtor-commission-commission-overhaul-nar — describes the traditional approximately 5% commission context and changes in the market.
- CaptivateIQ, “Average Sales Commission Rates by Industry in 2025”: https://www.captivateiq.com/blog/sales-commission-rates-by-industry — gives broad industry context and notes that high-ticket sales may use higher commission rates; it is not a SITUN-specific benchmark.
- BCG, “Shifting to an Automotive Direct-Sales Model”: https://www.bcg.com/publications/2023/shifting-from-selling-new-cars-to-earning-commissions-on-them — discusses automotive commission economics without prescribing a marketplace rate.

Proposed SITUN starting schedule for manual mediation, chosen to be lower than a full-service broker in high-value categories:

| Category | Seller commission | Buyer service fee | Rationale |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Immobilier | 2.5% | 0.5% | High ticket; keep entry fee modest and disclose before introduction |
| Automobile | 4% | 1% | Covers verification and buyer/seller coordination |
| Luxe | 8% | 2% | Higher curation and provenance work |
| Transport | 5% | 1% | Coordination and service matching |
| Other goods | 10% | 2% | More hands-on marketplace support |

All amounts are recorded manually. The website does not collect money automatically. Rates should be placed in seller agreements and buyer disclosures, and reviewed with a qualified local professional for each jurisdiction.
